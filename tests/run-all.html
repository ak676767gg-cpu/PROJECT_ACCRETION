    THE FLIGHT MANUAL

    Flight manual

    01

    Drift with intention.

    WASD or arrows apply thrust. Release to coast. Hold Shift to brake. Momentum matters: move slowly through a field to let gravity work.

    02

    Catch an orbit.

    Smaller objects curve toward you. Early captures spiral inward. At star scale, suitable trajectories can settle into stable orbits. Absorb bodies below about 63% of your simulation mass; similar bodies bounce and fragment.

    03

    Read the danger.

    Red markers indicate larger bodies. Build sideways velocity for a slingshot, then thrust outward. Large impacts damage cohesion; it regenerates after six quiet seconds. Hardcore caps cohesion at 50% and makes worlds scarcer as you grow. Rare cache-marked bodies carry extra DNA, water and Raw Material; collect them through contact or inner absorption.

    04

    Adapt and evolve.

    Each major evolution grants one point. Press U for three stackable choices. Unlock Space for escape burns, E for a gravity pulse, and Q for amplified gravity.

    Solar systems and matter

    At star scale, coast to preserve planets. Hold O for orbital paths. Tab toggles a wide system view; click a planet to select it. L opens the observatory. C collapses the selected orbit over time. Gas disks condense into planetesimals. Black holes collect matter in a disk before gradually accreting it. Seek nebulae and dense galactic arms.

    Life signals and incubation

    Look for H₂O and DNA markers on icy asteroids. Physical contact collects the marked resources once. Tidal breakup preserves them in the stream; collect them when it reaches your inner accretion region. A passing or escaping stream awards nothing. Press N for Life Lab. Use the discovery wheel to obtain an egg, spend 20–180 seconds in flight, then return to hatch it. Equip one of 50 companions to extend life-signal detection. Your collection survives new journeys. Equip a companion to give it a saved nickname in the Life Lab. Press H during flight to hide or restore the HUD and notifications; Escape still opens the pause menu.

    Save slots and companion bonuses

    Equipped companions grant rarity-based thrust, gravity and collision protection, listed in the Life Lab. Choose one of three save slots from the main or pause menu. Its toggle enables checkpoints every three evolutions; turn it off for regular timed autosaves. Save now also keeps all progress. Companion size is adjustable in Settings or the Life Lab.

    Orbital engineering: U

    The old random choices have become a deterministic tree. Earn one adaptation point at halfway and one at the end of every evolution bar. Points are banked before Moon scale, when the five main branches open: Mining, Science, Attack, Defense and Speed. Mining starts free: install Orbital Outpost for a station and one drone. Drones travel to intact rocks, cut them apart, and carry finite Raw Material home. Shots and mining fracture real mass into recoverable debris; ore is not regenerated by rebreaking it.

    Science: spend 25 Raw Material to build the Research Centre. Each powered batch consumes 50 Raw Material and returns one Research Point after 18 simulated seconds. Installed research and power upgrades shorten that time. Most ranks cost one adaptation point plus one Research Point. From rank three, or after ten powered research minutes, they cost two Research Points. Every row lists prerequisites and shows installed ranks. Science unlocks the other branches, advanced mining, optics, sample recovery, stellar engineering, Energy and Cosmetics.

    Attack builds turrets that prioritize rivals and otherwise fracture ore-bearing rocks. Optics and two turret ranks unlock attack lasers; mining lasers require an installed attack laser, two Mining Fleet ranks and Drill Assemblies. Defense adds shielding and existing damage reduction. Speed adds thrusters, counter-thrust, braking and the Space boost. All installations add visible modules to your body or station, including stellar and remnant forms.

    Energy: build closed-cycle turbines, fission reactors, fusion reactors, stellar collectors and Dyson swarms in sequence. Batteries store surplus power in Balanced mode. Charge mode diverts generation from mining, research, weapons and shield recharge; ordinary flight still works. Fleet Overclock spends stored power to speed up all powered equipment together. Short duration means higher intensity, up to 2.5×; flight gains at most 25%. Both sliders show the actual available-battery tradeoff. Timers stop in menus. Cosmetics adds decorative follower worlds with a visibility toggle; they carry no physical mass or capture bonuses.

    Orbital blueprints

    The observatory has five optional Blueprints. Assemble planets, moons, belts and period relationships through real captures, then coast through the uninterrupted hold. First completion earns the Architect crest. Survived impacts leave a capped history of craters or fractures; gas worlds show disturbed bands, and stellar bodies retain the record as plasma or disk imprints.

    Appearance and commemorative journeys

    Appearance lets you select earned body markings, companion accessories; Settings holds the unified interface theme. These have no gameplay bonuses. After reaching star scale and completing four different class journey goals in one run, Expeditions lets you retire your body and begin as an asteroid with an optional harder promise. Companions, eggs, resources, achievements and cosmetics carry over within the slot; your old body, orbits and adaptations retire. Rank rewards commemorate the journey without increasing your power. Ready eggs now open in a short reveal showing the actual creature, with a distinctive legendary sequence. Reveal companion skips to the result. Reduced motion displays it immediately.

    Selected evolution journeys

    At Massive Star, CHOOSE PATH unlocks in flight and pause. Selection keeps your current star, mass, momentum, health, adaptations and partly filled growth goal. Future stages follow your selected journey. Singularity: Contracting Supergiant, Collapsing Core, then Singularity and the existing black holes. Nebula: Mass-shedding Star, Expanding Stellar Shell, then Emission Nebula, Molecular Cloud, Stellar Nursery and Galactic Nebula. Neutron: Compressed Stellar Core, Proto-neutron Core, Small Neutron Star, Magnetar, then Strange Matter. Gravastar: Dense Shell Star, Vacuum-transition Core, then Compact, Vacuum and Cosmic Gravastar. A later starting star retains its current form.

    Path progression and remnant abilities

    Each new class requires five times the previous threshold. Revise a choice while still at the starting star stage; the first branch evolution locks it. EVOLUTION PATH then shows the reached and upcoming stages. Supernova and special abilities arrive when the first remnant is reached, after the two precursor classes. Singularity doubles gravity and moves at two-thirds speed. Nebula has no gravity and slowly dissolves overlapping matter. Neutron stars gain 35% speed; Magnetar adds 50% gravity. Strange Matter gains a conversion field and 25% speed. Gravastars gain 25–60% gravity and 40–55% collision protection, moving at 90% speed. The actual supernova impulse can disturb nearby orbits. Ignoring the choice keeps normal stellar growth through Stellar Colossus. These compressed transitions retain mass and do not model real stellar mass loss.

    Living horizons

    Each black-hole class has its own procedural silhouette: ember lanes, a tilted copper vortex, polar jets, a golden torus, a galactic maelstrom, a quasar crown and the Interstallar. Bright knots travel around the disk; inner lanes turn faster. Stored captured matter controls gas brightness and jet activity. Faint returned-light arcs can appear without stored matter. The Interstallar has separate moving outer filaments. Pause and Photo freeze all flow; Reduced Motion holds the optional paths still, and Performance lowers their detail.

    The Interstallar

    The Interstallar is the final evolution after the ultramassive black hole. Its pale inner ring and amber outer disk carry moving filaments; inner lanes turn faster. Accretion brightens the flow. Your mass can keep growing after the final class. Reduced motion holds the rings still, and pausing or taking a photo freezes them. To inspect this class immediately, use the separate Sandbox and press B for experiments.

    Inspired by astronomy, designed for play. Mass labels follow astronomical landmarks; size, time, distance, and mass ratios are compressed.     The Final Interstallar

    Actual black-hole forms can discover a distant persistent horizon. Follow the gold marker or press G to plot its course; 8 hides its approach instrument. Beacon, lens, disk and photon zones lead to an irreversible inner-flow warning. Turn outward early with full thrust; Shift brakes. Defense and emergency shell still work. An early black hole cannot merge.

    At The Interstallar or greater black-hole scale, slow below 38 scale-relative units per second between 1 and 3 shadow radii and hold for 1.5 seconds. The protected merger takes ten active, visible seconds; Escape pauses it. The two masses combine once, then flight continues. Charting and completing make explicit checkpoints. Sandbox placements award no journey advancements. In Photo mode, choose 1× or 2× export; PNG width is capped at 3840 pixels.

    Galactic encounters and FPS

    Press G after reaching star scale and choose Join encounter. Four phases last thirty minutes each, counted only during visible active flight. Follow the bridge course to gather its finite gas and condensed worlds. Completion banks three adaptation points up to the 100-point cap, increases gravity by 10% and gas intake by 20% for this journey. Its clock is saved every minute without changing your chosen flight checkpoint cadence. FPS in telemetry measures rendered frames over half a second. Hidden tabs are excluded. Reduced Motion freezes decorative galactic rotation.

    ABOUT ACCRETION

    ACCRETION

    A space evolution game with offline journeys, scenario modes and an optional private multiplayer arena.

    Black holes in this game are an original real-time rendering inspired by published scientific visualisation. No film footage, images or sound are included.

    Every surface, nebula, star, particle, and sound is generated locally. No external art, music, fonts or runtime libraries. Offline modes make no network requests. Optional multiplayer connects only to the server address you enter.

    Built with JavaScript, Canvas 2D, CSS, and the Web Audio API. See the sources and implementation notes for the scientific references and local source files.

    Mass labels use astronomical reference points. Distances, elapsed time, growth rates and gravitational interactions are compressed for gameplay. Companions are fictional lifeforms.
